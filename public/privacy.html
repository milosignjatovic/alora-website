Privacy policy

How Alora handles what you record about your health.

Draft. This page is being prepared ahead of Alora's release and is not yet in force. It will be completed and reviewed before the app is available in the stores.

The short version

What you log about your health stays on your phone. Only a meal photo you choose to scan is sent to an AI service. You decide what to share and with whom.

Who we are

To be added before release: the company responsible for Alora (the data controller), its address and a contact for privacy questions.

What stays on your phone

Your health log, such as symptoms, digestion, meals, medicines, plans and notes, is stored on your device.

You can save a backup as a file; you choose where that file is kept, and Alora has no copy of it.

To be added before release: what an optional account stores off the device, and where.

Meal photos

When you scan a meal, the photo is sent to a third-party AI service that names the food on your plate. Only the photo is sent: none of your entries and no part of your profile go with it. Alora does not keep the photo. The first time you scan, the app tells you this and asks before anything is sent.

To be added before release: the name of the service, where it processes photos, and how long it keeps them.

Reports you share by link

When you share a doctor's report by link, the report is encrypted on your phone before it leaves it, and the key travels only inside the link. Alora stores only the encrypted file, cannot open it, and deletes it when the link expires or you stop sharing. Anyone who has the link can open the report until then.

Usage data

Alora asks once whether it may collect anonymous usage data, such as which screen an entry was started from. It is off until you agree, it never includes what you logged, and you can turn it off in Settings.

To be added before release: the name of the analytics service.

Your rights

Your log is on your phone, so you can read, correct, export and delete it yourself, in the app.
